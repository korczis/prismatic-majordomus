## 107. Completion discharges obligations and never decides whether an intent is satisfied

### Context

ADR 0070 made an intent a typed record whose stage is derived from the plan and whose
satisfaction is derived from the evidence ledger. Its first consequence planned the opposite
direction for later: once completion policy landed (pull request 292, cited there as ADR 0057,
which exists only on an unmerged branch), whether an intent is satisfied would become a
completion-policy question over the same evidence, and the intent engine would answer the
stage only. Slice 2 of the `intent-lifecycle` milestone carries that plan.

Completion answers a different question. `majordomus finish` judges whether a task discharged
the obligations its profile and policy declare: scope, verification, the note, the regression
test, the gates. An intent asks whether something is observably true for the people it is for.
A task can be finished and its intent still false, and an intent can become true without any
task finishing — a test that starts passing, a claim whose inputs stop changing. If completion
policy decided satisfaction, a finished task would be one policy edit away from stamping an
intent satisfied, which is the merge-stamping ADR 0070 rejected under another name.

The engine couples two answers today: `intent::stage` returns `satisfied` only when every
counted milestone is DONE and every criterion is met, so a reader cannot ask whether the
evidence alone settles an intent while its milestones are still open.

### Decision

- **One authority.** Whether a satisfaction criterion is met is decided by the intent engine
  (`apps/majordomus-cli/src/intent.rs`) over the evidence ledger and the claim join, and by
  nothing else. No other module, policy file, capability or gate computes, stores or overrides
  a criterion's verdict or an intent's satisfaction.
- **Completion reads, never decides.** Completion policy decides whether work is done. It may
  read an intent's derived standing as an input to a question (for example, to report which
  criteria a task advanced), but it never declares a question whose answer is that an intent is
  satisfied, never writes an intent record, and never stores a stage or a verdict. A finish
  with outcome `completed` leaves every intent's stage and criterion states exactly as the plan
  and the evidence derive them.
- **The stage stays plan-derived.** `stage` keeps its meaning: `satisfied` is the stage of an
  intent whose counted milestones are DONE and whose criteria are all met; no surface changes
  here.
- **Satisfaction gets its own verdict.** A separate derived verdict answers the evidence
  question alone: `satisfied` when every criterion is met, `unsatisfied` when a criterion the
  ledger can settle is not met, `unknown` when the unmet criteria are only of kinds the ledger
  cannot yet settle (`command`, `deployment`). DONE neither implies it nor is required for it.
  The field is named `verdict`, because `satisfaction` already names the criteria array in the
  record and in `IntentView`. Slice S07 implements it, with drift findings where the verdict and
  the stage disagree.
- **ADR 0070's first consequence is superseded** by this decision, and slice 2 of the
  `intent-lifecycle` milestone is restated to match.

### Alternatives rejected

**Satisfaction as a completion-policy question (ADR 0070's plan).** It would put a policy file
between the evidence and the answer, and a finished task would become a route to a satisfied
intent. Completion is about the task; satisfaction is about the world the task was for.

**Keeping satisfaction coupled to DONE.** It is true of today's stage, but it hides an answer
the evidence already has: an intent whose criteria all hold while a milestone is open is
exactly the drift a reader needs to see, and a coupled answer cannot show it.

**Renaming the existing `satisfaction` array to free the name.** It is in the schema, the
distribution skeleton, the documentation examples and every surface's output; renaming it
breaks readers to save choosing a new word.

### Consequences

- `project.completion-never-decides-satisfaction` states the boundary, and
  `test/cases/895_a_finished_task_does_not_satisfy_an_intent.sh` proves it through the built
  executable: a completed finish next to a DONE milestone with unmet criteria leaves the stage
  `verifying`, the criterion `not_run` and the intent record unchanged.
- The completion batch's policy (`share/completion.yaml`) must never gain an
  intent-satisfaction question; a question that reads intent standing is an input, never a
  verdict.
- S07 adds `verdict` to `IntentView` on every surface; until it lands, the stage is the only
  derived answer and it stays coupled to DONE.
- Evidence-first acceptance at the plan transition — `plan done` on an issue that serves a
  criterion requiring that criterion's evidence — is not decided here; it belongs to the plan,
  not to completion, and is left to a later slice.
