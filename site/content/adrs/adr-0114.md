## 114. A batch is composed by the integrator, names its members, and is one candidate like any other

### Context

ADR 0101 lists batches under the alternatives it rejected: "They trade attribution for
throughput, and a red batch costs a person a bisection. They remain a person's decision, not
the executor's." The rule that keeps ADR 0101 from drifting back says in its rationale that the
subsystem replaced "hand-built batches whose regressions nobody could attribute".

On 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-08 the person's decision was taken many times over, by hand. One
pull request lands per CI run of its refreshed head; a run costs about ninety minutes; and
every landing makes every other open pull request stale against master. With twelve pull
requests open, a session acting as integrator composed five `int/batch-*` branches by hand:
`git merge --no-ff` of each member's head, one version bump, one derive, one pull request,
merged by `prs drain --max 1`. Sixteen pull requests reached master in three such landings
(#806, #815, and #816 in flight). No required check was bypassed and each batch was decided
against the master it landed on. What the hand-built form did not have is what ADR 0101
objected to, measured on those five:

- **No record of composition.** Which members a batch carried, at which heads and in what
  order, existed in a pull request's prose and in the composer's notes. Nothing a tool could
  read.
- **Authored commits on the batch itself.** Four of the five carried fixes written on the
  batch branch (a renamed heading, a case moved to the foreground, a budget, two migrated
  cases). A regression in one of those belongs to no member, and a member's own pull request
  never saw the fix.
- **No eligibility rule.** A member went in because its owner said it was green. #739 went
  into a batch with 331 uncovered changed items and one failing case; #818 was offered with
  two pages missing; #821 without a site build.
- **No refusal.** Nothing in CI tells a composed branch from any other, so nothing stopped
  the practice the rule's rationale names, and nothing would stop a careless one.

The owner's decision of 2026-10-08, given the choice between forbidding batches and making
them a typed capability with attribution: make them a capability of the subsystem, enforced,
documented and visible on every surface.

### Decision

#### D1. A batch is one pull request, and it is the integrator that composes it

`majordomus prs compose` plans a batch from the queue; `--apply` builds it. A batch is an
ordinary pull request against the base: it is classified, ranked, repaired, merged and
verified by the code that handles every other pull request, and the one-merge-at-a-time
invariant of ADR 0101 is untouched. `prs drain` merges one pull request and plans again;
when that pull request is a batch, several members' work lands in the one merge.

Composition is not a merge into master and takes no decision about master. It is the act
`prs repair` already performs for one pull request, performed for several: in a scratch
worktree of the executor's own, under the base branch's integration lease, recorded on the
trail before anything reaches the remote.

#### D2. Membership is decided, not asserted

A pull request is eligible for a batch only when all of these hold on a fresh observation:

- it is open, targets the base, is not a draft, carries no blocking label, and its head lives
  in this repository;
- the review policy is satisfied on its current head;
- every required check passed on its current head, as the classifier's `required_checks` and
  `no_failing_check` gates judge it and with nothing judged a second time here. Its head may
  be behind master: that a member need not be refreshed and re-run alone is the throughput a
  batch buys, so the `freshness` gate is the one gate a member may fail. A check that is
  pending, missing, unreadable, or written by the wrong app is not passed; a skip the
  repository's CI plan permits passes, as it does for any pull request (ADR 0101, unchanged);
- auto-merge is not armed on it, and the repository allows a merge commit: a member the
  forge would merge on its own, or a batch that could only be squashed, is not composed;
- every dependency it declares has landed, or is a member placed before it;
- it is not itself a batch, and no open batch already names it.

Members are taken in the planner's rank order, up to `integration.batch.max_members` of the
policy (no default in code; `--max` may lower it and never raise it). A member whose merge
into the composition conflicts on an authored path is left out, named with the paths, and
the composition goes on without it. A conflict on a `merge=derived` path is not a conflict:
the derive settles it. That needs the `merge.derived` driver, which is per-clone
configuration; in a clone without it the act fails whole and names the driver, and resolves
nothing by hand.

A composition of fewer than two members is not a batch and is refused: one pull request is
`prs repair`.

#### D3. The manifest is the batch's identity, and it is tracked

`--apply` writes `.ai/repo/integration/batches/<id>.yaml`, of kind `integration-batch/v1`:
the base master it was composed on, the version before and after the composition's bump,
and for each member, in order, its number, its head, its title and the merge commit that
carries it. The id is the base master's short name followed by the member numbers in
composition order, so the same members on the same master are the same batch, and composing
them twice is refused by the branch that already exists. The manifest is committed on the batch branch,
lands with the batch, and is a declared object of the layer like any other: it is indexed,
served, and read by `prs explain` and by the Cockpit's queue. It is published as no page of
its own yet: a manifest has no prose body for the generic object page to show, and a page
that lists a batch's members is a template this decision does not include.

The batch's pull request body carries one `Supersedes #N` line per member. Under ADR 0101
D3, written by the executor's account in a pull request of this repository, that is an
authorised declaration: each member is held while the batch is open, released if the batch
closes without landing, and closable by `prs cleanup --apply` once git finds the batch's
merge commit in master.

#### D4. Attribution is structural

A batch branch consists of, on its first-parent line from the base master and in this order:
one merge commit per member, whose second parent is that member's recorded head; then at
most one commit of the composition itself, which may change only the manifest, the version
files `release bump` writes, and paths that are `merge=derived`. Nothing else. A fix is
written on the member's branch, and the batch is composed again.

So a regression found on master after a batch landed is located by `git bisect
--first-parent` over the batch's own range and lands on one member's merge commit, or on the
composition commit, which no person wrote. `prs explain` names a batch's members in order,
and on a member names the open batch that carries it.

Reading a red batch's failed jobs and naming the member whose paths they mention is not
decided here. It would be evidence and never a verdict, and nothing is built on it yet.

#### D5. A composed branch that is not a batch is refused

A gate of the structure job, `batch-check`, reads the pull request under test. If its head
contains the heads of two or more other open pull requests of this repository, it must carry
a manifest whose members are exactly the second parents of its first-parent merges, in
order, and its composition commit must touch nothing D4 forbids. Otherwise the gate refuses
it, naming the member it found and the manifest line it did not.

A branch that merges one other pull request is a stack, not a batch, and is not this gate's
subject, with one exception: a branch that adds or changes a manifest is a batch whatever it
merges, and is judged as one. Otherwise a batch one of whose members moved after composition
would count one member merge, pass as a stack, and carry a manifest nobody read. The gate
needs the forge to know which pull requests are open; where it cannot read it, it answers
that it could not run, never that the branch is clean.

#### D6. A batch never lands what its members did not earn

`release bump` on the composition is taken once, to what the public contract of the composed
tree requires. A member carries no bump of its own into a batch. The release rule of
2026-10-08 (a release carries less payable debt than the one before) is judged on the
batch's tree like on any other.

#### D7. Rollout

Dry run first: `prs compose` with no flag is a read, decided offline from the last recorded
observation, like `prs status`. `--apply` is refused on two grounds, each said by name:
while this decision's status is not `accepted`, read from the layer, because composing is
what the decision permits and accepting it is a person's act; and until the trail holds one
verified merge since the last merge that could not be verified. ADR 0101 §13 gates only the
continuous drain and names no threshold for a bounded one, so this is a threshold of this
decision's own. There is no continuous composition: a batch is composed when a person, or a
session acting for one, asks for it.

Neither refusal is an act and neither is written to the trail: nothing was selected and
nothing attempted. The trail begins at `compose_selected`.

If the pull request cannot be opened after the branch was pushed, the branch exists with no
proposal; the act records `compose_refused` saying so, and a second run is refused by the
branch until a person opens the pull request or deletes it. The check that the branch does
not exist and the push are two steps, not one: two executors are kept apart by the lease,
not by that check.

### Consequences

- The practice of 2026-10-07/08 becomes a command with a record, or is refused. The five
  hand-built batches predate the gate and are named in its evidence, not grandfathered by a
  baseline: they are merged, and the gate reads open pull requests.
- A member's owner fixes on the member's branch. That is slower by one composition than
  fixing in the batch, and it is the cost of knowing who changed what.
- ADR 0101's rejected alternative is amended, not reversed: a batch remains a person's
  decision to ask for. What changes is that the executor builds it, the layer records it and
  CI can tell it from a hand-built one.
- Eligibility needs each member's own CI verdict, so a member that was never run alone
  cannot ride a batch. #745's first real verdict came inside a batch; that cannot recur.

### Alternatives rejected

- **Keep batches a hand act with a written workflow.** A document nothing reads is what the
  five batches already had.
- **Forbid batches.** Twelve open pull requests at ninety minutes each, serially, with a
  repair and a derive between: the owner asked for integration often.
- **Let the executor batch on its own in a drain.** A batch trades a member's independence
  for throughput; that trade is asked for, not inferred.
- **Squash a batch into one commit.** It erases the member merges that make a bisection land
  on a member.
- **Record membership only in the pull request body.** A body is a person's to edit and the
  forge's to lose; the manifest is tracked, typed and lands with the code.
